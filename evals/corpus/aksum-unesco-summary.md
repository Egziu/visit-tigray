Aksum was inscribed on the UNESCO World Heritage List in 1980. The site is the ruins of the ancient city of Aksum, near Ethiopia's northern border, which was the heart of ancient Ethiopia when the Kingdom of Aksum was a major power in trade between the Roman Empire and India.

The ruins include monolithic obelisks (stelae), giant stelae, royal tombs and the ruins of ancient castles. The tallest stelae were carved from single blocks of granite and decorated to represent multi-storey buildings.

The Kingdom of Aksum minted its own coins and became one of the first states to adopt Christianity, in the fourth century CE, during the reign of King Ezana.

Long after its political decline, Aksum remained an important religious centre. Ethiopian Orthodox tradition holds that the Ark of the Covenant is kept at the Church of St Mary of Zion in Aksum; this is a matter of faith and tradition, and visitors cannot view it.
